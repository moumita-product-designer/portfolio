Account setup banner: turning a scattered verification process into one clear step

How a single, status-driven banner helped HCPs and patients get verified, set up payments, and start earning in the ZoomRx Community app.

The problem

In 2024, ZoomRx was recruiting new HCPs and patients into its Community panel for market research and the new TV Tracker. New members had to complete three things before they could earn: verify their email, verify their identity (or, for patients, their diagnosis), and set up payments. These steps were spread across the dashboard, the menu and follow-up emails, and nothing tied them together.

The FY24 lessons documented by the Media Tracking team described it plainly: the signup process “required multiple, disjointed steps, meaning that it was unclear to users what needed to be done to get verified and claim their earnings,” with an “over-reliance on text-based instructions and emails” and copy that was “stylistically inconsistent.” Patients in particular were dropping off before completing signup.

The product: ZoomRx's survey app

ZoomRx is a life sciences consulting firm. Pharma and biotech companies hire it to understand how doctors and patients think about their treatments, and much of that research comes from ZoomRx's own panel: a community of healthcare professionals and patients who answer research surveys.

The ZoomRx Community app, the survey app, is where that panel lives. Members sign up, get verified, take paid surveys they qualify for and get paid for their time. Patients can also join the TV Tracker, which helps pharma brands understand how their TV campaigns reach the people they're meant for.

  1. JoinAn HCP or patient signs up to the Community panel on web or mobile.
  2. Get verifiedConfirm their email, then prove who they are: an HCP's credentials, or a patient's diagnosis.
  3. Take surveysPaid research surveys matched to their specialty or condition appear on their home screen.
  4. Get paidAn honorarium for each completed survey, paid out through PayPal, bank transfer or Zelle.

Why it matters to ZoomRx

The panel is ZoomRx's own data source. It's the healthcare community that every market research product the company sells is built on, and one of the things competitors can't easily copy. A panelist only becomes useful to that research once they're verified and set up to be paid. Anyone who drops out during setup never takes a single survey.

That made onboarding the front door to the whole business. In 2024 the panel was growing fast, with HCP signups passing 50,000, but only about 1 in 5 members were actively taking part. Every new member who got stuck between signing up and their first paid survey was supply ZoomRx had worked to recruit and then lost.

What I designed

I turned the old verification banner into a single Account Setup banner that holds every step a new member needs to finish, for both HCPs and patients, on desktop and mobile. The idea was simple: one place, clear status, and no guessing about what is left to do.

Key design decisions

DecisionWhy it mattered
Email, identity and payment setup in one bannerPayment setup was the step most often missed; bringing it into the same place as verification made it part of “setting up your account” instead of an afterthought.
Steps can be completed in any orderUsers no longer got stuck behind one slow step, such as waiting on identity review.
A clear status for every stepEach tab moves from Not started to In progress, Under review and Completed, shifting from orange to green, so users always know where they stand.
Expanded until done, then collapsedThe banner opens expanded on first visit and every return until everything is submitted, then collapses to stay out of the way.
A clear finish lineAn “Account Setup Complete” state, an in-app notification and a push notification replace the old silence after verification.
Payment nudges where earning happensA $ icon with a red dot until payment is set up, a reminder in the TV Tracker, and a “you earned your first $1” pop-up that links straight to payments.
Patient diagnosis verification in the bannerPatients can upload proof per indication (with a $5 bonus for each verified indication) and manage diagnoses from their profile.
Smart defaults for fast-track usersMembers who joined through a pre-verified link see identity already marked Completed.
Rewritten copyPlain, consistent language for each step and for what happens after payment details are submitted.

The flows

ZoomRx Community has two kinds of panelists, HCPs and patients, and each has its own onboarding flow and its own UI. Both use the same Account Setup banner pattern: three steps in one card, each with its own status, completable in any order, with every failure routed back to the screen where it can be fixed.

In both flows, the $ icon with a red dot in the navigation bar is a visual indicator that the user hasn't set up payment yet. It stays on every screen until payment setup is done.

The UI, screen by screen

Desktop screens from the final HCP design, in the order an HCP meets them. The three-step header stays fixed at the top of the card, so HCPs always see where they are and what's left.

01 · Entry & visibility
Screen 01

Expanded on first visit

The card sits at the top of the home screen, above the surveys, and opens already expanded. One progress header holds all three HCP steps (Email Verification, Identity Verification and Payment Setup), so a new member sees the whole job at once.

The email step says where the link was sent, what to do if it hasn't arrived (check spam, or write to info@zoomrx.com) and offers a Resend Verification Link button. That guidance used to live in follow-up emails. A “Have any questions or concerns?” box gives a support route on every step.

In the navigation bar, the $ icon with a red dot is a visual indicator that the user hasn't set up payment yet. It stays on every screen until payment setup is done.

Screen 02

Collapsed, never hidden

The chevron folds the card down to just the three-step header. The tabs keep their colours, so progress is still readable while the user browses surveys.

The red dot on the $ icon stays too, so even with the card collapsed, the user can see that payment setup is still pending. Until everything is submitted, the card opens expanded again on every visit.

02 · Email verification
Screen 03

Email confirmed

Once the link is clicked, the status chip changes to Complete, the tab turns green with a check, and the copy confirms it: “Email verification successful! Please proceed to the next step.”

Before the redesign, verification ended in silence. Now every step ends with a visible confirmation and a Next button to the following step.

03 · Identity verification
Screen 04

Two ways to prove you're an HCP

Identity Verification starts at Not Started. A two-option toggle lets the HCP verify with a professional (office or organisation) email or upload a medical license.

A checkbox covers the common case where the professional email is the same as the contact email. A note underneath explains that research communications still go to the contact email, answering the privacy question before it's asked.

Screen 05

Upload a medical license

For HCPs without a professional email, the same card switches to a single upload field and a Verify Me button.

The layout, status and button stay where they were, so changing methods doesn't feel like starting a different flow.

Screen 06

Under review, with a timeframe

After submitting, the status changes to Under Review and the tab turns orange. The copy sets expectations: “Please allow up to 2 business days for your credentials to be verified.”

Because steps can be done in any order, Next stays available. The HCP can go on to payment instead of waiting on review.

Screen 07

Identity approved

When the review is approved, the tab turns green and the status reads Complete. Two of the three tabs now carry a check, so it's obvious only payment is left.

04 · Payment setup
Screen 08

Choose how to get paid

Payment used to be the step people missed, so it now sits in the same header as verification. Three payout methods appear as pills: PayPal, ACH Transfer and Zelle.

For PayPal, a “Use my Contact email” checkbox fills in the address the HCP already gave, saving a step. The Set Up PayPal Account button only turns green once there's an email to submit.

Screen 09

Bank transfer details

Choosing ACH Transfer swaps in the bank form: name, account and routing numbers, bank name and address. Required fields are marked.

It's the longest form in the flow, so it keeps the same card, status chip and support box as every other step.

Screen 10

Payment set up

With payment confirmed, all three tabs are green and the card says “Payment set up successfully! Welcome to ZoomRx.”

The red dot on the $ icon is gone, because payment setup is no longer pending.

05 · Completion
Screen 11

Steps in any order

Here email is done, identity is still in review (orange) and the HCP is already setting up PayPal.

No step blocks another, so nobody gets stuck waiting on identity review before they can set up how they'll be paid. The card stays visible until every step is submitted.

Screen 12

Account Setup Complete

When every step is done, the card collapses into a single line: “Account Setup Complete”, with a green check. This is the finish line that was missing before, backed by an in-app notification and a push notification.

Screen 13

The card disappears

After that, the setup card and the red dot on the $ icon both disappear. The top of the home screen goes back to community content and surveys, with nothing left to chase.

The UI, screen by screen

Desktop screens from the final patient design, in the order a patient meets them. The three-step header stays fixed at the top of the card, so patients always see where they are and what's left.

01 · Verification dropdown
Screen 01

Expanded on first visit

Patients get their own three steps: Email Verification, Diagnosis Verification and Payment Setup. For first-time patients the card opens expanded at the top of the home screen, starting with email.

The email step explains where the link went, what to do if it hasn't arrived, and offers a Resend Verification Link button. In the navigation bar, the $ icon with a red dot is a visual indicator that the patient hasn't set up payment yet.

Screen 02

Collapsed, reopen any time

Closing the card leaves the three-step header in place, so the patient can still see their progress and reopen it at any time. The red dot on the $ icon stays until payment is set up.

02 · Email verification
Screen 03

Email confirmed

Once the patient follows the secure link, the status turns Complete, the tab turns green and the copy confirms it. Diagnosis Verification becomes the next step.

03 · Diagnosis verification
Screen 04

My Conditions

Instead of proving who they are, patients prove what they have. The step opens on My Conditions, where each condition the patient reported has its own card, proof field and Verify button.

Examples of accepted proof (medication labels, medical bills, lab or test results) sit right above the upload, so patients know what to send before they try. “Earn $5 for verifying conditions with active studies” and an Active Study tag on the card answer the old question of which study a proof bonus was for.

Screen 05

Add or remove a condition

Before the redesign, patients had no way to add or remove a diagnosis. Now Add Diagnosis opens a searchable list; the patient picks a condition and taps Add, and it appears with its own proof field.

The minus icon on a condition card removes it, so the list always matches what the patient actually has.

Screen 06

Attach proof and verify

Attached files show as chips in the proof field, with a count when there are several (“+3”), and each can be removed before submitting.

The Verify button only becomes active once there's something to send, so patients can't submit an empty upload.

Screen 07

Under review, condition by condition

After submitting, the condition moves up into My Conditions with an Under Review tag and a +$5 marker, and the note “Allow 2 to 5 business days for proof to be verified.” The step status changes to In-Progress and the tab turns orange.

The Unverified Conditions area stays below, so the patient can keep adding conditions or uploading proof while one is being reviewed.

Screen 08

Each condition keeps its own status

Here Plaque Psoriasis is Verified and Leukaemia is still Under Review, each with its +$5 marker. Once a condition is verified, the step is marked Complete and the tab turns green.

One slow review doesn't hold up the rest of setup, and the patient can still see exactly where every other condition stands.

Screen 09

When every condition is removed

If a patient removes all their conditions, the card doesn't dead-end. The proof examples and the $5 incentive stay, and Add Diagnosis becomes a solid green button: the one obvious next action.

04 · Payment setup
Screen 10

Choose how to get paid

Patients choose between PayPal, ACH Transfer and Zelle. For PayPal, “Use my Contact email” offers the email they already gave, shown as a chip under the checkbox, so they don't have to type it again.

Set Up PayPal Account stays grey until there's an email to submit.

Screen 11

Bank transfer (ACH)

ACH Transfer swaps in the bank form: name, account and routing numbers, bank name and address, with required fields marked.

Screen 12

Zelle

Zelle asks for the patient's name, the email or phone number linked to their Zelle account, and their address. Each method gets only the fields it needs, inside the same card.

Screen 13

Payment under review

After submitting, the payout details go under review. The tab turns orange and the copy explains the wait: “Please allow up to 2 business days for your changes to be applied and for your payment to reflect in your chosen account.”

This answers a pain point from research: patients didn't know what happened after they submitted payment details.

Screen 14

Payment set up

Once confirmed, all three tabs are green and the card says “Payment set up successfully! Welcome to ZoomRx.” The red dot on the $ icon is gone, because payment setup is no longer pending.

05 · Completion
Screen 15

Account Setup Complete

With every step done, the card collapses into a single “Account Setup Complete” line with a green check. The patient gets a clear finish line, backed by in-app and push notifications.

Screen 16

The card disappears

Then the setup card and the red dot on the $ icon disappear for good, and the home screen goes back to community content and surveys.

Where onboarding stands today (2026)

The account setup banner is still in the app today, now in a redesigned form. Since 2024, the identity step has added a face scan. The Community team's 2026 panel analysis shows where new HCP members drop off now, and it points back to the same principle this project was built on: the fix is a simpler flow.

“Flow redesign is the fix, not chasing latency.”

Community team panel analysis, Jul 2026

The 2026 analysis locates today's drop-off at a newer, higher-friction step, the face scan. That is the kind of problem the 2024 banner addressed: when every step is visible, clearly explained and easy to finish, more people get through it.

What I learned

Designing for a funnel means designing for the moment someone doesn't know what to do next. Putting every step in one place, showing progress honestly and celebrating the finish did more than any extra email reminder could.

I'd also attach success metrics before launch next time. The team tracked the release qualitatively, and the Design team adopted a rule in Nov 2024 that every release needs a success metric defined before launch. A database query comparing verification and payment-setup rates for signups before and after Aug 13, 2024 is ready to run, to put exact numbers on this impact.
